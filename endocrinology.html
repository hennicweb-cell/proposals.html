Understanding the Society

A learned society and a commercial subsidiary, moving into its next phase

Thank you for taking the time to speak with me. As we discussed, you've moved away from the traditional agency model and found CharityJob works well for you, and my approach is most cost-effective for more senior appointments. Rather than describe it in the abstract, I've built this proposal around your live vacancy.

Established in 1946 and marking its 80th anniversary this year, the Society brings together clinicians, nurses, scientists and associated professionals, with 2,743 members recorded in the 2025 annual report. Its trading subsidiary, Bioscientifica, provides publishing, events and association management services, and its commercial activity helps finance the Society's charitable work. The two are closely connected but retain distinct governance responsibilities.

2025 was a year of significant change. A new four-division structure replaced one that had grown organically and produced silos and duplicated effort; the Executive Team reduced from six roles to five; the former management tier was removed; and the overall salary bill fell by approximately £550,000. The published accounts show group income of £5.29m, expenditure of £5.29m, readily available reserves of £3.3m against a £2.3m target, and a Bioscientifica profit of £837,000 before Gift Aid, which was £148,000 ahead of budget.

That is a steadier footing, but broadly break-even rather than comfortably in surplus. The trustees identify dependence on publishing, pharmaceutical sponsorship and registry income as risks, so the next challenge is to turn the Society's clinical expertise and networks into sustainable programmes, stronger member engagement and partnership income.

"To strategically lead the Society's clinical programmes, research portfolio and partnership activities, strengthening the Society's influence, impact and sustainability..." — the purpose of the role, in your own words.

This vacancy sits directly at that intersection, and it's recruitment into a changed organisation rather than simple business-as-usual succession.

The strategic purpose of the role

More than a clinical programmes appointment

The job description gives clinical leadership, research and partnerships equal weight, with organisational leadership as a fourth strand. It reads as a senior leader who connects clinical credibility, research delivery and commercial partnerships, rather than a specialist in any one of them.

The post carries a budget of around £1m a year and leads a team of four through two direct reports, the Clinical Programme Manager and the Head of Partnerships & Corporate Sales. It needs someone who sets direction while staying close enough to delivery to make a lean team effective.

The recruitment challenge

Why this looks like a harder-than-usual appointment to fill

The brief asks for clinical-sector credibility, research literacy and commercial judgement in roughly equal measure, plus the ability to influence through member-led governance rather than hierarchical authority. At senior level, people commonly bring one or two of these strongly. Finding all of them in one person, who is also comfortable leading in a recently reorganised, lean organisation, is the real difficulty.

The likely pool is also wider than a conventional clinical-programmes shortlist, and each source is strong on some pillars and needs testing on others:

  • Medical royal colleges, specialist societies and professional bodies — strong on programmes and committee governance; test direct ownership of income
  • Medical research and health charities — strong on research portfolios and funding partnerships; test experience of serving professional members
  • NHS and university research partnerships — strong on research governance; test commercial delivery and working in a lean organisation
  • Life-sciences and healthcare partnership leaders — strong on industry relationships and negotiation; test independence and member-led culture
  • Real-world evidence and registry programme leaders — strong on data-led, industry-funded research; test breadth across member engagement and executive leadership

Notably, the person specification doesn't state that the appointee must be a doctor or registered clinician. That is worth confirming together early, so it doesn't become an unspoken screening requirement that narrows the field unintentionally. Partnership growth also has to sit alongside research governance, data protection and the integrity of pharmaceutical relationships, which is why the assessment needs to test commercial judgement carefully, not simply sales experience.

Why this model could suit the Society

How I'd approach this search

A four-stage process

Clarifying the brief

A joint briefing to confirm the context of the role (replacement, expanded remit or redesigned post), first-year priorities, income accountability, how much of the research remit is registries versus grants and collaborations, whether clinical qualification is essential, and what the director inherits in the team.

Advertising and targeted mapping

The role advertised on CharityJob, with structured mapping and direct approaches across royal colleges, specialist societies, research charities, NHS and university partnerships, and life-sciences and registry leaders. Early calibration across three archetypes: clinical-programmes leader, research-collaboration leader and healthcare-partnerships leader.

Evidence-led assessment

Structured interviews testing candidates against all three pillars together, with a candid briefing on the restructuring, resources and income expectations. A scenario-based final exercise, for example evaluating a proposed industry-funded clinical or registry initiative: strategic value, governance, finances, stakeholder engagement and delivery.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of three to five appointable candidates with written assessments against the brief, plus support through interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management.
