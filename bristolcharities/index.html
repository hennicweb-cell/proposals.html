Why an advert alone has limits

Only around 30% of the market is looking at any one time

At any given moment, roughly 30% of potential candidates are actively searching: reading job boards, setting up alerts and applying. Your advertisement will reach them, and some will be excellent.

30%Actively looking
Visible on job boards. This is who an advert reaches.
70%Not looking, but open to the right opportunity
In post, performing well and rarely on job boards. This is where much of the real quality lies.

The other 70% are the people who are doing well where they are. They're running payroll accurately month after month and supporting managers with HR, so they have no reason to browse vacancies. They'll only hear about Bristol Charities and Props if someone they can trust approaches them directly, explains the mission honestly and makes a considered case for why a role is worth a conversation.

That's the gap my approach is designed to fill, whenever it's useful to you. I keep an advertisement on CharityJob working, and then search the 70% directly, so the shortlist reflects the whole market rather than only the part of it that happens to be looking this month.

The search challenge

Dependable payroll and the appetite to build something new

The brief asks for someone who can be relied on to run payroll to a fixed deadline every month, and who also enjoys helping two organisations introduce a new system and shared practices. People who are strong at one often need testing on the other, and the role is jointly employed, so candidates need to be comfortable serving two charities with different cultures. In practice I'd look across four pools:

  • HR and payroll officers and coordinators in charities and small organisations — used to covering both disciplines; test experience of a system implementation
  • Payroll specialists from finance teams and bureaus — strong on accuracy, HMRC and pensions; test breadth across day-to-day HR
  • HR administrators who have also run payroll in housing, care or education settings — close to the sectors both charities work in; test Excel depth and parallel-run testing
  • Candidates who have been through an HR or payroll system change — bring data migration and training experience; test day-to-day payroll reliability

Because the role handles sensitive pay and personal data, and because Props supports adults with learning disabilities, I'd give particular attention to discretion, fairness and respect in how candidates deal with colleagues. I'd make sure candidates from a wide range of backgrounds hear about the role, and that every conversation is open, fair and consistent.

How I'd approach this search

A transparent, five-stage process

You would see how the search is progressing at every stage, and you'd have a documented, defensible process to show your colleagues and trustees.

Foundation and briefing

A briefing that goes beyond the job description. I'd agree with you what success looks like at 6 and 12 months, the system rollout and who leads which parts, how time is shared between the two charities, the essential experience and where you're open to transferable backgrounds, and how the role works with the Head of Resources and managers. I'd also agree with you the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate.

Talent mapping and outreach

A structured mapping of the four pools, concentrating on the 70% who aren't on job boards. Each person is approached directly with an honest account of Bristol Charities, Props and the role. Any advertisement continues alongside, and anyone who responds to it is assessed against the same framework.

Weekly transparency

I'd meet you weekly throughout. You'd see the talent pool as it's built: who has been identified, who has been approached, who has shown interest, and who has been ruled out and on what basis. Nothing happens behind closed doors, and you can challenge or redirect the search at any point.

Candidate assessment

I interview every candidate I progress against the agreed competencies, testing payroll accuracy, Excel and reconciliation skills, HR administration, discretion and system implementation experience through real examples. You receive a written report on each, covering strengths, risks and development areas. Where helpful, a short practical exercise, such as a payroll reconciliation using fictional data, never unpaid consultancy.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of three to five candidates who are appointable on both capability and fit. I then support interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management, and stay in touch with the successful candidate before they start.
