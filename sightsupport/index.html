Understanding Sight Support

The local sight loss charity, building a stronger local fundraising base

Thank you for speaking with me, and for being so candid about how the recruitment has gone. You said this round has been difficult and that you can only keep doing the same things and getting the same results for so long. This proposal is about doing something different, built around your live vacancy.

Sight Support West of England is the local sight loss charity for Bristol, BANES and South Gloucestershire, providing face-to-face information, advice and guidance for blind and partially sighted people. It works in partnership with Insight Gloucestershire and Wiltshire Sight, three separate charities sharing central functions, including a Head of Fundraising, Marketing and Communications, to deliver services cost-effectively.

This new post is there to increase your capacity on the ground: developing local high-street, community and corporate relationships, and recruiting and supporting the volunteers who bring them to life. It is the kind of role where a local, trusted face makes a real difference to income.

The search challenge

Why this kind of role can be hard to fill

The brief asks for a rare mix: the warmth and persistence to build local relationships and lead volunteers, the commercial confidence to pitch to opticians, supermarkets and local businesses, and the organisation to manage events and income. The role is also local, so candidates need to live within reach of Bristol, BANES or South Gloucestershire, and be able to drive, which narrows the field further. The people best suited are often busy in other jobs and not scanning job boards, which is why a standard advert alone can keep producing the same results. In practice I'd look across four pools:

  • Community and events fundraisers at local charities — strong on volunteers, events and local networks; test the appetite for corporate relationships
  • Corporate and partnerships officers — strong on pitching and account management; test enthusiasm for grassroots, volunteer-led work
  • Volunteer managers and coordinators — strong on recruiting and motivating people; test fundraising instinct and income tracking
  • Local relationship builders from other sectors — for example retail, optical, hospitality or community engagement; test how they'd get up to speed in fundraising

As a sight loss charity, you particularly welcome applications from people who are visually impaired. I'd make sure the process is fully accessible from the first conversation, in the format each candidate prefers, so that nobody is put off before they've had a chance to show what they can do. The wide salary band and the willingness to consider part-time working are real strengths in widening the field, and I'd make sure candidates hear about both.

How I'd approach this search

A transparent, five-stage process

You would see how the search is progressing at every stage, and you'd have a documented, defensible process to show your Board and colleagues across the three charities.

Foundation and briefing

A joint briefing that goes beyond the job description. Together we'd agree what success looks like at 6, 12 and 24 months, the essential experience and the areas where you're open to transferable backgrounds, the values and style that matter at Sight Support, how the role works with the Volunteer Co-ordinator and the wider fundraising team, and how to position the role for people who aren't looking. We'd also agree the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate.

Talent mapping and outreach

A structured mapping of the four pools across Bristol, BANES, South Gloucestershire and the surrounding area. Each person is approached directly, with an honest account of Sight Support, the role and what it offers. The role is also advertised on CharityJob, but the emphasis is on proactive contact with people who haven't seen it.

Weekly transparency

We'd meet weekly throughout. You'd see the talent pool as it's built: who has been identified, who has been approached, who has shown interest, and who has been ruled out and on what basis. Nothing happens behind closed doors, and you can challenge or redirect the search at any point.

Candidate assessment

I interview every candidate I progress against the agreed competencies, testing relationship-building, volunteer leadership, local credibility and motivation for the cause. You receive a written report on each, covering strengths, risks and development areas. Every conversation is held in an accessible format.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of three to five candidates who are appointable on both capability and fit, and who understand the charity and the reality of the job. I then support interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management, and stay in touch with the successful candidate before they start.
