Understanding Caring in Bristol

Uniting Bristol to end homelessness

Thank you for letting me introduce myself. I fully respect that you have your own process for this appointment, and I'm not asking you to change anything. I've put this together so you can see how I work, in case it's ever useful.

Caring in Bristol is a local charity with a clear mission: uniting Bristol to end homelessness. Over the last five years you've built a vibrant local brand and a diverse mix of income streams, and the charity now raises around £1.3m a year. The strategy has moved towards preventing homelessness, through direct support, community empowerment and system change, and demand has grown, with a reported 51% rise in people seeking help in 2025–26.

What stands out to me is that this is a team-building role at the heart of the senior leadership team. The Head of Fundraising is accountable for all of the charity's income, across individual giving, trusts and grants, corporate partnerships, community and events fundraising, and a new major donor and legacy programme, and is asked to build a team and a culture where fundraisers can do their best work, in line with your trauma-reflective approach.

Why an advert alone has limits

Only around 30% of the market is looking at any one time

At any given moment, roughly 30% of potential candidates are actively searching: reading job boards, setting up alerts and applying. Your advertisement will reach them, and some will be excellent.

30%Actively looking
Visible on job boards. This is who an advert reaches.
70%Not looking, but open to the right opportunity
In post, performing well and rarely on job boards. This is where much of the real quality lies.

The other 70% are the people who are doing well where they are. They're growing income and leading fundraising teams where they are, so they have no reason to browse vacancies. They'll only hear about Caring in Bristol if someone they can trust approaches them directly, explains the mission honestly and makes a considered case for why a role is worth a conversation.

That's the gap my approach is designed to fill, whenever it's useful to you. I keep an advertisement on CharityJob working, and then search the 70% directly, so the shortlist reflects the whole market rather than only the part of it that happens to be looking this month.

The search challenge

A leader, a team-builder and a major donor fundraiser in one

The brief asks for someone who has led multi-stream fundraising at a comparable scale, has personally asked for and secured major gifts, and has built or rebuilt a team after a period of change. People who are strong at one or two of those often need testing on the third, and the three days a week in the Bristol office shapes who can apply. In practice I'd look across four pools:

  • Heads and Directors of Fundraising in Bristol and South West charities of a similar size — know the local landscape and multi-stream leadership; test major donor experience and team rebuilding
  • Major gifts and philanthropy leads ready to step up to a head of function — strong on cultivation and asking; test team leadership and budget ownership
  • Fundraising leaders from homelessness, housing, social justice and community charities — close to the cause; test major donor and legacy depth, and appetite for the Bristol base
  • Senior fundraisers with experience of rebuilding teams after change — including interim and consultancy backgrounds; test fit with a trauma-reflective culture and the long-term commitment

Because the role works with people with lived experience of homelessness, I'd give particular attention to dignity, warmth and respect in how candidates talk about supporters and beneficiaries. I'd make sure candidates from a wide range of backgrounds hear about the role, and that every conversation is open, fair and consistent.

How I'd approach this search

A transparent, five-stage process

You would see how the search is progressing at every stage, and you'd have a documented, defensible process to show your colleagues and trustees.

Foundation and briefing

A briefing that goes beyond the job description. I'd agree with you what success looks like at 6, 12 and 24 months, the shape of the team the postholder will lead and which posts are in place, the starting point for the major donor and legacy programme, the essential experience and where you're open to transferable backgrounds, and how the role works with you, the Co-Directors and the fundraising trustee. I'd also agree with you the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate.

Talent mapping and outreach

A structured mapping of the four pools, concentrating on the 70% who aren't on job boards. Each person is approached directly with an honest account of Caring in Bristol and the role. Any advertisement continues alongside, and anyone who responds to it is assessed against the same framework.

Weekly transparency

I'd meet you weekly throughout. You'd see the talent pool as it's built: who has been identified, who has been approached, who has shown interest, and who has been ruled out and on what basis. Nothing happens behind closed doors, and you can challenge or redirect the search at any point.

Candidate assessment

I interview every candidate I progress against the agreed competencies, testing multi-stream income leadership, major donor asking and stewardship, team building, forecasting and board reporting, and alignment with your values and trauma-reflective approach, through real examples. You receive a written report on each, covering strengths, risks and development areas. Where helpful, a short practical exercise, such as outlining a first-year major donor plan from a fictional scenario, never unpaid consultancy.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of three to five candidates who are appointable on both capability and fit. I then support interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management, and stay in touch with the successful candidate before they start.
