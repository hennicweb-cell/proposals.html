The vacancy

Fundraising and Communications Officer

A hands-on role that combines building a well-evidenced funder pipeline with telling the charity's story, in a small team.

Why an advert alone has limits

Only around 30% of the market is looking at any one time

At any given moment, roughly 30% of potential candidates are actively searching: reading job boards, setting up alerts and applying. Your advertisement will reach them, and some will be excellent.

30%Actively looking
Visible on job boards. This is who an advert reaches.
70%Not looking, but open to the right opportunity
In post, performing well and rarely on job boards. This is where much of the real quality lies.

The other 70% are the people who are doing well where they are. They're writing winning funding applications, building donor relationships and telling compelling stories, so they have no reason to browse vacancies. They'll only hear about Learning with Parents if someone they can trust approaches them directly, explains the mission honestly and makes a considered case for why a role is worth a conversation.

That's the gap my approach is designed to fill, whenever it's useful to you. I keep an advertisement on CharityJob working, and then search the 70% directly, so the shortlist reflects the whole market rather than only the part of it that happens to be looking this month.

The search challenge

A rare combination of fundraising craft and compelling communications

The brief asks for someone who can research prospects and write persuasive funding proposals, and also produce blogs, case studies and an impact report that make the charity visible. People who are strong at one often need testing on the other, and the salary range and hybrid pattern shape who will be interested. In practice I'd look across four pools:

  • Trusts and foundations fundraisers ready to step up — strong on prospect research and proposal writing; test storytelling and appetite for digital content
  • Fundraising and communications officers in education and children's charities — close to the sector and to schools; test pipeline discipline and corporate or major donor experience
  • Communications and content professionals moving into fundraising — strong on writing, WordPress, Canva and social; test their grasp of funders and income targets
  • Candidates with lived experience of the barriers disadvantaged families face — bring credibility and insight; test the fundraising craft side of the brief

Your commitment to a team that reflects the families you serve matters here too. I'd make sure candidates from the groups you particularly welcome hear about the role, and that every conversation is open, fair and consistent. I'd also be mindful of your safeguarding requirements and your AI in recruitment policy, so that my process sits comfortably alongside yours.

How I'd approach this search

A transparent, five-stage process

You would see how the search is progressing at every stage, and you'd have a documented, defensible process to show your colleagues and trustees.

Foundation and briefing

A briefing that goes beyond the job description. I'd agree with you what success looks like at 6, 12 and 24 months, the current funder pipeline and CRM, the balance between fundraising and communications, the essential experience and where you're open to transferable backgrounds, and how the role works with you and the Marketing Lead. I'd also agree with you the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate.

Talent mapping and outreach

A structured mapping of the four pools, concentrating on the 70% who aren't on job boards. Each person is approached directly with an honest account of Learning with Parents, the role and the salary range. Any advertisement continues alongside, and anyone who responds to it is assessed against the same framework.

Weekly transparency

I'd meet you weekly throughout. You'd see the talent pool as it's built: who has been identified, who has been approached, who has shown interest, and who has been ruled out and on what basis. Nothing happens behind closed doors, and you can challenge or redirect the search at any point.

Candidate assessment

I interview every candidate I progress against the agreed competencies, testing writing and editing, prospect research, funder relationships, project management and alignment with your values through real examples. You receive a written report on each, covering strengths, risks and development areas. Where helpful, a short practical exercise, such as drafting a short funder update from a fictional scenario, never unpaid consultancy.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of three to five candidates who are appointable on both capability and fit. I then support interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management, and stay in touch with the successful candidate before they start.
