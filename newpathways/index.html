Understanding New Pathways

Specialist support for survivors, and a growing organisation to sustain it

Thank you for taking the time to read this. We haven't been in touch before, so I wanted to introduce myself properly. New Pathways provides specialist support for people affected by rape, sexual assault and sexual abuse across Wales, alongside training, education and research relating to trauma. Its services include counselling and wellbeing support, crisis support and Independent Sexual Violence Adviser services, and the charity reports supporting more than 11,000 survivors each year, with children and young people making up over a quarter. A sexual violence support centre opened in Newport in 2025.

The latest published accounts, for the year to March 2025, show a growing and well-run organisation: income of around £4.84m, up from about £4.12m, a modest surplus, and a mix of charitable activities income, grants, contracts, donations and trading. That variety is exactly what makes the Business Development Manager role interesting: it has to find the right opportunities across commissioning, grants, tenders and partnerships, and turn them into income that is properly costed and genuinely deliverable.

Why an advert alone has limits

Only around 30% of the market is looking at any one time

At any given moment, roughly 30% of potential candidates are actively searching: reading job boards, setting up alerts and applying. Your advertisement will reach them, and some will be excellent.

30%Actively looking
Visible on job boards. This is who an advert reaches.
70%Not looking, but open to the right opportunity
In post, performing well and rarely on job boards. This is where much of the real quality lies.

The other 70% are the people who are doing well where they are. They're winning contracts, writing successful bids and building commissioner relationships, so they have no reason to browse vacancies. Bid and business development specialists who understand Welsh funding are a small, well-networked group, and they'll only hear about New Pathways if someone they can trust approaches them directly, explains the opportunity honestly and makes a considered case for a conversation.

That's the gap my approach is designed to fill, whenever it might be useful to you. I keep an advertisement on CharityJob working, and then search the 70% directly, so the shortlist reflects the whole market rather than only the part of it that happens to be looking this month.

The search challenge

Turning a strong specialist proposition into sustainable, well-costed income

The brief spans grants, contracts, tenders and partnerships, with a communications strand and a small team to lead, in a Welsh funding landscape where local knowledge counts. A broad wish list can obscure what the person will actually spend their time doing, so one of the first questions would be which income channel matters most in the first year. In practice I'd look across four pools:

  • Business development and bid managers in Welsh charities — strong on VAWDASV, health and social care funding; test leadership and communications range
  • Commissioning and tender specialists from health, mental health and social care — strong on procurement and public-sector relationships; test the move into a specialist survivor-support charity
  • Income-generation professionals with both bid-writing and relationship skills — strong on mixed-income charities; test Welsh policy and funder knowledge
  • Strong deputies ready for broader responsibility — strong on quality and detail; test pipeline ownership and external representation

I'd keep the requirements proportionate and clarify early whether Welsh-language skills are essential or desirable. I'd test for sustainable contribution rather than gross income won: full-cost recovery, realistic delivery budgets, funding restrictions and renewal risk, and whether a candidate has the judgement to match opportunities to operational capacity. Because of the nature of the work, I'd also be open with candidates about the sensitivity of the service and the safeguarding checks involved.

How I'd approach this search

A transparent, five-stage process

You would see how the search is progressing at every stage, and you'd have a documented, defensible process to show your trustees.

Foundation and briefing

A joint briefing that goes beyond the job description. Together we'd agree what success looks like at 6 and 12 months, which income channels come first, the targets and existing pipeline the postholder will inherit, who owns bid writing, pricing and contract negotiation, how the communications strand fits, the team and support available, whether Welsh-language skills matter, and how existing applicants are handled. We'd also agree the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate.

Talent mapping and outreach

A structured mapping of the four pools across Wales, the South West and beyond, concentrating on the 70% who aren't on job boards. Each person is approached directly with an honest account of New Pathways, its work and the role. Any advertisement continues alongside, and anyone who responds to it is assessed against the same framework.

Weekly transparency

We'd meet weekly throughout. You'd see the talent pool as it's built: who has been identified, who has been approached, who has shown interest, and who has been ruled out and on what basis. Nothing happens behind closed doors, and you can challenge or redirect the search at any point.

Candidate assessment

I interview every candidate I progress against seven areas: income development, relationship management, bids and propositions, commercial judgement, pipeline management, internal collaboration, and mission and judgement. You receive a written report on each. For finalists I'd suggest a short fictional scenario, not a free strategy: a service tender with a short deadline, a request for bespoke training, and a grant that only partly matches priorities, asking which they'd pursue, what they'd need to know, and how they'd involve service and finance colleagues.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of three to five candidates who are appointable on both capability and fit, with salary expectations and notice periods. I then support interview design, candidate preparation, references, and offer management alongside your safeguarding and pre-employment checks, and stay in touch with the successful candidate before they start.
