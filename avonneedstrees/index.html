Understanding Avon Needs Trees

A young, ambitious charity creating permanent woodland for the Bristol-Avon catchment

Dave, thank you for taking a moment to read this. I'd like to introduce myself, and I want to be clear that I respect your own recruitment process. I'm not asking you to change anything. This is simply a short proposal built around your Head of Development role, so you can see how I work, in case it's ever useful.

Founded in 2019 by a small band of volunteers, Avon Needs Trees buys land to create woodlands that will stand for generations, locking up carbon, boosting biodiversity and providing natural flood management and public green space. From two sites in Wiltshire, through the Great Avon Wood partnership with the Forest of Avon Trust, to the 420-acre Lower Chew Forest, described as the biggest new woodland in the South West in a generation, the growth has been remarkable, with more than 2,000 volunteers behind it.

The next phase brings new land purchases and the launch of commercial activities at Lower Chew Forest, including Biodiversity Net Gain, alongside a fundraising strategy built on corporate partnerships, individual giving (crowdfunders in particular, where you have a strong record), philanthropists, and trusts and foundations. Under your leadership, more than £10m has been raised cumulatively for land acquisition, which is a strong foundation to build on.

Why an advert alone has limits

Only around 30% of the market is looking at any one time

At any given moment, roughly 30% of potential candidates are actively searching: reading job boards, setting up alerts and applying. Your advertisement will reach them, and some will be excellent.

30%Actively looking
Visible on job boards. This is who an advert reaches.
70%Not looking, but open to the right opportunity
In post, performing well and rarely on job boards. This is where much of the real quality lies.

The other 70% are the people who are doing well where they are. They're winning corporate partners, building pipelines and leading teams, so they have no reason to browse vacancies. For a role that welcomes commercial business-development backgrounds as well as charity fundraisers, that pool is wide. They'll only hear about Avon Needs Trees if someone they can trust approaches them directly, explains the mission honestly and makes a considered case for a conversation.

That's the gap my approach is designed to fill, whenever it might be useful to you. I keep an advertisement on CharityJob working, and then search the 70% directly, so the shortlist reflects the whole market rather than only the part of it that happens to be looking this month.

The search challenge

A player-manager who wins income, not just plans it

The role asks someone to generate new business personally, lead two fundraising leads, support a specialist colleague on commercial opportunities and contribute to the senior leadership team, all while being committed to the mission. That combination is rare, and the fixed term to March 2028 asks candidates to weigh up the security of their current roles, so it's best to talk about it openly from the first conversation. In practice I'd look across five pools:

  • Corporate fundraising and partnerships leaders — strong on securing and growing business relationships; test breadth across philanthropy and individual giving
  • Philanthropy and major-donor fundraisers with wider income experience — strong on high-value relationships; test corporate and commercial confidence
  • Development leaders in environmental, conservation and place-based charities — close to the mission; test pipeline discipline and conversion
  • Senior fundraisers at smaller charities — used to combining strategy with personal delivery; test appetite for a leadership and commercial remit
  • Commercial business-development professionals — strong on relationship-led sales and targets; test their grasp of charitable fundraising, supporter relationships and ethical income generation

I'd keep the requirements proportionate. The essential criteria prioritise income generation and relationship-building, so I wouldn't require deep technical nature-market knowledge if it excludes strong candidates. I would test whether candidates understand the difference between a donation, a corporate sponsorship and a commercial transaction, and can describe environmental impact credibly without overstating what a partnership or purchase achieves.

How I'd approach this search

A transparent, five-stage process

You would see how the search is progressing at every stage, and you'd have a documented, defensible process to show your trustees.

Foundation and briefing

A joint briefing with you and the Director of Income that goes beyond the job description. Together we'd agree what success looks like at 6 and 12 months, the income targets and current baselines, how much time the postholder spends fundraising personally versus managing, the strengths of the two fundraising leads, the split of responsibility with the Natural Capital & Nature Markets Lead, and how existing applicants are handled. We'd also agree the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate.

Talent mapping and outreach

A structured mapping of the five pools across the South West and beyond, concentrating on the 70% who aren't on job boards. Each person is approached directly with an honest account of Avon Needs Trees, its ambitions and the role, including the fixed term. Any advertisement continues alongside, and anyone who responds to it is assessed against the same framework.

Weekly transparency

We'd meet weekly throughout. You'd see the talent pool as it's built: who has been identified, who has been approached, who has shown interest, and who has been ruled out and on what basis. Nothing happens behind closed doors, and you can challenge or redirect the search at any point.

Candidate assessment

I interview every candidate I progress against seven areas: income growth, relationship development, planning and forecasting, proposition development, stewardship and CRM, team leadership, and mission and judgement. For each I'd distinguish gross income from net contribution, restricted from unrestricted support, and team results from the candidate's own contribution. You receive a written report on each. For finalists I'd suggest a short fictional case, not a free strategy: prioritising a mix of corporate, philanthropic and individual-giving opportunities, outlining the first 90 days, and explaining how they'd share the work with two fundraising leads.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of three to five candidates who are appointable on both capability and fit, with salary expectations and notice periods. I then support interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management, and stay in touch with the successful candidate before they start.
