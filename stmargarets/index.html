Understanding St Margaret's

Compassionate care for Somerset, on foundations built over 45 years

Thank you for taking the time to look at this. I fully appreciate that you are exploring your own avenues for this appointment and may already have suitable candidates. I'm not trying to interfere with that. I wanted to introduce myself, and this proposal shows how I'd approach the search, in case it's ever useful.

For more than 45 years, St Margaret's has provided specialist palliative and end-of-life care, free of charge, to people across Somerset, a population of around 520,000. That care is enabled by 344 colleagues, 66 bank staff and 1,480 volunteers, with annual turnover of more than £16 million, two hospice sites and 32 charity shops. The five-year strategy, Time to Care – Now & Always, focuses on raising the hospice's voice, enhancing care and stewarding resources, and the latest published report describes a flexible care model in which the great majority of care is delivered outside an inpatient building, through outpatient services, day centres and people's homes.

The hospice's own reporting describes a sound financial position and strong reserves, together with a funding model that is naturally volatile, depending on legacies, fundraising, trading and one-off capital funding, and on a significant share of costs that must be raised locally. That makes this an appointment about protecting resilience while enabling the strategy: helping the Chief Executive and Board make confident choices about services, investment, infrastructure and long-term sustainability.

Why an advert alone has limits

Only around 30% of the market is looking at any one time

At any given moment, roughly 30% of potential candidates are actively searching: reading job boards, setting up alerts and applying. Your advertisement will reach them, and some will be excellent.

30%Actively looking
Visible on job boards. This is who an advert reaches.
70%Not looking, but open to the right opportunity
In post, performing well and rarely on job boards. This is where much of the real quality lies.

At director level the effect is even stronger. The other 70% are finance leaders who are doing well where they are, running budgets, leading teams and advising boards, so they have no reason to browse vacancies. They'll only hear about St Margaret's if someone they can trust approaches them directly, explains the role honestly and makes a considered case for a conversation.

That's the gap my approach is designed to fill, whenever it might be useful to you. I keep an advertisement on CharityJob working, and then search the 70% directly, so the shortlist reflects the whole market rather than only the part of it that happens to be looking this month.

The search challenge

A finance leader with real operational breadth

The advert combines a qualified, commercially minded finance director with hands-on leadership of the estate, facilities and procurement, and asks for someone who is credible with clinicians as well as trustees. People strong in finance often lack estates and operational depth, and those with operational breadth may lack the financial grip. The role is also based in Taunton or Yeovil with regular travel across Somerset, which needs to be discussed with candidates early. In practice I'd look across five pools:

  • Finance and Resources Directors at hospices and care charities — close to the model; test fresh thinking and the scale of the estate agenda
  • Finance Directors in health, disability, social care and supported living — strong on multi-site estates and complex operations; test understanding of charitable funding volatility
  • Charity finance leaders who have run procurement, facilities and capital investment — test depth in each, not nominal responsibility
  • NHS and health-sector finance leaders — strong on scale and governance; test appetite for a mixed-income charity model and retail economics
  • Heads of Finance and Resources ready for an executive step up — test executive influence and sufficient scale

I'd also keep the requirements proportionate. I wouldn't restrict the search to existing hospice directors, and I'd confirm early exactly what sits within "resources", such as IT, HR or company secretarial duties, so the specification doesn't narrow the field unintentionally. The right candidate will distinguish recurring performance from restricted capital funding, legacy volatility and investment movements, and show how financial decisions affect patients, colleagues, volunteers and supporters.

How I'd approach this search

A transparent, five-stage process

You would see how the search is progressing at every stage, and you'd have a documented, defensible process to show your Board.

Foundation and briefing

A joint briefing with the Chief Executive and relevant trustee representative that goes beyond the advert. Together we'd agree what success looks like at 6, 12 and 24 months, the exact remit and direct reports, how the estate review fits the future care model, the essential evidence and where you're open to transferable backgrounds, and how existing applicants are handled. We'd also agree the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate.

Talent mapping and outreach

A structured mapping of the five pools across Somerset, the South West and beyond, concentrating on the 70% who aren't on job boards. Each person is approached directly with an honest account of St Margaret's, the strategy and the role, including the location and travel. Any advertisement continues alongside, and anyone who responds to it is assessed against the same framework.

Weekly transparency

We'd meet weekly throughout. You'd see the talent pool as it's built: who has been identified, who has been approached, who has shown interest, and who has been ruled out and on what basis. Nothing happens behind closed doors, and you can challenge or redirect the search at any point.

Candidate assessment

I interview every candidate I progress against seven areas: financial strategy, financial resilience, estates and resources, commercial judgement, governance and assurance, executive leadership and people leadership. For each I'd establish what they personally led, the scale involved, the decisions they made and the results. You receive a written report on each. For finalists I'd suggest a fictional case study, not an unpaid estate strategy: a volatile income forecast, rising workforce costs, a choice between two capital investments and a mixed-performing retail portfolio, asking how they'd weigh the risks, protect reserves and present options to trustees.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of three to five candidates who are appointable on both capability and fit, with salary expectations and notice periods. I then support interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management, and stay in touch with the successful candidate before they start.
