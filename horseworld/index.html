Understanding HorseWorld

Horses given a second chance, helping young people find theirs

Thank you for taking the time to speak with me, and for being open to seeing how I work. Rather than describe my approach in the abstract, I've built this proposal around your live vacancy.

HorseWorld has been rescuing, rehabilitating and rehoming horses, ponies and donkeys from Keynes Farm in Whitchurch since 1952, and reaches its 75th anniversary in 2027. Alongside the welfare work, Discovery, your equine-assisted learning programme, supports children and young people facing educational, emotional and social challenges. That pairing of animal rescue and human impact is an unusually strong story for a communications leader to work with.

The search challenge

A wide brief and a small pool of people who want all of it

The job description is clear that you don't expect a candidate to tick every box, and that you'd consider both an ambitious charity marketer ready for more autonomy and an experienced commercial marketer seeking a cause. That openness is a strength, but it means the search has to reach people who aren't actively looking at charity vacancies, and test them carefully on the things that matter. In practice I'd look across three pools:

  • Charity marketers and fundraising communications specialists — strong on supporter journeys, appeals and regular giving; test appetite for the breadth of the remit and for managing agencies
  • Commercial and agency marketers seeking a mission-led move — strong on audience insight, creative quality, conversion and commercial discipline; test understanding of charity fundraising and long-term stewardship
  • Marketers in education, youth, animal welfare or outdoor learning — already close to the cause or to Discovery; test breadth across acquisition and brand

There are a few practical points worth exploring together early. The title says "Fundraising" but the focus is acquisition, engagement and campaign delivery, so candidates need to see that clearly. The role is based on site in Whitchurch, so expectations about time at the farm need to be explicit, and a candidate's first-year ambitions should be matched to the budget. Candidates will also be curious about the reporting line to an interim head, so a clear account of how that leadership will develop will help them make a confident decision.

How I'd approach this search

A four-stage process

Clarifying the brief

A joint briefing to agree how I can best help alongside your live campaign: supplementing it, taking over screening, or running a revised search. We'd confirm the leadership context and who the post reports to, why the recruitment is happening now, first-year priorities, budgets and agency roles, supporter baselines, weekly hours on site, how existing applicants are handled, and who approves the shortlist and fee.

A candidate proposition and targeted approach

A short candidate proposition built on HorseWorld's dual impact, animals and young people, and the freedom and ownership the role offers. The role advertised on CharityJob, alongside direct approaches to marketers across all three pools.

Evidence-led screening

Structured conversations testing campaign results, writing and storytelling, conversion thinking, agency and team management, and genuine motivation for HorseWorld. A written candidate report on each shortlisted person. Where helpful, a proportionate hypothetical-campaign exercise, for example how they'd approach a Big Give or regular-giving challenge, designed so it never becomes unpaid consultancy.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of appointable candidates with written assessments against the brief, plus support through interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management. All communication with candidates is personally managed by me, so they feel well looked after throughout.
