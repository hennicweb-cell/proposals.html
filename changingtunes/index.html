Changing Tunes began in 1987 when its founder took a guitar into HMP Bristol. It now works across 18 locations in England and Wales, in prisons, pupil referral units and children's secure homes, offering long-term music and mentoring through musicians-in-residence, and continuing that support after release. Its reoffending rates are significantly below the national average, and the 2026–31 strategy sets out a confident next phase: amplifying young voices, strengthening the musician-in-residence model and expanding post-release community music pathways.

"Changing Tunes has been a bright light at the end of a very long tunnel. It's made me realise I am still me inside." — a participant, quoted in your recruitment pack.

Why an advert alone has limits

Only around 30% of the market is looking at any one time

At any given moment, roughly 30% of potential candidates are actively searching: reading job boards, setting up alerts and applying. Your advertisement will reach them, and some of them will be excellent.

30%Actively looking
Visible on job boards. This is who an advert reaches.
70%Not looking, but open to the right opportunity
In post, performing well and rarely on job boards. This is where much of the real quality lies.

The other 70% are the people who are doing well where they are. They're building income, leading teams and getting results, so they have no reason to browse vacancies. They'll only hear about Changing Tunes if someone they can trust approaches them directly, explains the mission honestly and makes a considered case for why this role is worth a conversation.

That is the gap my approach is designed to fill. I keep your advertisement on CharityJob working, and then search the 70% directly, so the shortlist reflects the whole market rather than the part of it that happens to be looking this month.

The search challenge

A senior brief at 0.6 FTE, in a sensitive and distinctive field

The role spans fundraising, communications, brand, digital and AI, team leadership, board reporting and strategic planning, in three days a week. The appointee has to be genuinely senior, commercially disciplined and comfortable prioritising, while staying hands-on enough to deliver income. The cause also needs the right person: someone who can build a compelling public narrative around criminal justice and trauma with care and credibility. In practice I'd look across four pools:

  • Heads and senior managers at small and medium charities — ready for a step up in autonomy and influence; test strategic range across income streams and communications
  • Experienced part-time and portfolio fundraisers and consultants — used to senior work in fewer days; test leadership of a team and long-term ownership
  • Fundraisers in criminal justice, rehabilitation, arts and trauma-informed charities — already close to the mission; test breadth across trusts, individual giving and legacies
  • Individual giving and trusts leaders in larger charities — strong on data and performance; test appetite for a smaller, more hands-on organisation

Part-time senior roles are a strength as well as a challenge. They attract exceptional people who want mission-led work on flexible terms, but those people are rarely visible on job boards, which is exactly why direct approaches matter. I'd also test how candidates would diversify income beyond government contracts, how they'd improve retention and growth in individual giving and trusts, and how they'd introduce AI and data tools ethically in a small team.

How I'd approach this search

A transparent, five-stage process

You would see how the search is progressing at every stage, and you'd have a documented, defensible process to show your trustees.

Foundation and briefing

A joint briefing that goes beyond the job description. Together we'd agree what success looks like at 6, 12 and 24 months, the essential experience and where you're open to transferable backgrounds, the leadership style and values that matter at Changing Tunes, and how the role works with the CEO, trustees and existing fundraising team. We'd also agree the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate, and how the role is positioned for people who aren't looking.

Talent mapping and outreach

A structured mapping of the four pools, concentrating on the 70% who aren't on job boards. Each person is approached directly with an honest account of the charity, the strategy and the role. Your advertisement continues alongside, so nothing is lost, and anyone who responds to it is assessed against the same framework.

Weekly transparency

We'd meet weekly throughout. You'd see the talent pool as it's built: who has been identified, who has been approached, who has shown interest, and who has been ruled out and on what basis. Nothing happens behind closed doors, and you can challenge or redirect the search at any point.

Candidate assessment

I interview every candidate I progress against the agreed competencies, testing strategic range, income results across streams, leadership, data and AI judgement, and commitment to the mission. You receive a written report on each, covering strengths, risks and development areas.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of three to five candidates who are appointable on both capability and fit, and who understand the charity and the realities of a 0.6 FTE senior role. I then support interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management, and stay in touch with the successful candidate before they start.
