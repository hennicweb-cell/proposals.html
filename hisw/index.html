Transforming lives through innovation in health and care

You are one of 15 Health Innovation Network organisations established by NHS England in 2013 to spread innovation at pace and scale. A team of around 65 colleagues works across Somerset, Devon, Cornwall and the Isles of Scilly, connecting the NHS, universities, local authorities, the third sector and industry, with a particular focus on rural and coastal health. What stands out to me is how much this role is asked to do. Why an advert alone has limits

Only around 30% of the market is looking at any one time

At any given moment, roughly 30% of potential candidates are actively searching: reading job boards, setting up alerts and applying. Your advertisement will reach them, and some will be excellent.

30%Actively looking
Visible on job boards. This is who an advert reaches.
70%Not looking, but open to the right opportunity
In post, performing well and rarely on job boards. This is where much of the real quality lies.

The other 70% are the people who are doing well where they are. They're running campaigns and building reputations where they are, so they have no reason to browse vacancies. They'll only hear about Health Innovation South West if someone they can trust approaches them directly, explains the mission honestly and makes a considered case for why a role is worth a conversation.

That's the gap my approach is designed to fill, whenever it's useful to you. I keep an advertisement on CharityJob working, and then search the 70% directly, so the shortlist reflects the whole market rather than only the part of it that happens to be looking this month.

The search challenge

National ambition, a regional base and a focused team

The brief asks for a marketer who can build a national reputation in a specialist field, lead a small team and support business development, in a role based in Exeter within a £40–50k range. That combination, together with a requirement that candidates don't need sponsorship, narrows the field, so it's worth being honest about it from the start. The flexibility you offer on hours and part-time working helps widen it. In practice I'd look across four pools:

  • Marketing and communications managers in NHS bodies, integrated care boards and health innovation organisations — know the health system and its language; test campaign and data rigour and appetite for team leadership
  • Marketing managers in universities, research and life sciences organisations — used to thought leadership and complex audiences; test their understanding of NHS and care settings
  • Senior marketers in health and social care charities and social enterprises in the South West — mission-led and used to small teams; test business development marketing and national profile-building
  • Experienced marketers returning from a career break, or working part-time or flexibly — a pool your flexible approach opens up; test the currency of their digital and data skills

Because your process uses the Applied platform to minimise bias, I'd be glad to work with it, encouraging candidates to apply that way if you prefer. I'd screen for the right to work in the UK without sponsorship from the first conversation, make sure candidates from a wide range of backgrounds hear about the role, and keep every conversation open, fair and consistent.

How I'd approach this search

A transparent, five-stage process

You would see how the search is progressing at every stage, and you'd have a documented, defensible process to show your colleagues and trustees.

Foundation and briefing

A briefing that goes beyond the job description. I'd agree with you what success looks like at 6, 12 and 24 months, including the expectations around Horizon 3 and a national reputation, the balance between campaigns and business development support, the essential experience and where you're open to transferable backgrounds, and how the role leads the Digital Marketing and Events and Engagement Leads. I'd also agree with you the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate.

Talent mapping and outreach

A structured mapping of the four pools, concentrating on the 70% who aren't on job boards. Each person is approached directly with an honest account of Health Innovation South West and the role. Any advertisement continues alongside, and anyone who responds to it is assessed against the same framework.

Weekly transparency

I'd meet you weekly throughout. You'd see the talent pool as it's built: who has been identified, who has been approached, who has shown interest, and who has been ruled out and on what basis. Nothing happens behind closed doors, and you can challenge or redirect the search at any point.

Candidate assessment

I interview every candidate I progress against the agreed competencies, testing integrated campaign delivery, audience insight and data, brand management, business development support, people leadership and your values of being improvement focused, collaborative and impact-led through real examples. You receive a written report on each, covering strengths, risks and development areas. Where helpful, a short practical exercise, such as outlining a campaign plan from a fictional scenario, never unpaid consultancy.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of three to five candidates who are appointable on both capability and fit. I then support interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management, and stay in touch with the successful candidate before they start.
