Why an advert alone has limits

Only around 30% of the market is looking at any one time

At any given moment, roughly 30% of potential candidates are actively searching: reading job boards, setting up alerts and applying. Your advertisements will reach them, and some will be excellent.

30%Actively looking
Visible on job boards. This is who an advert reaches.
70%Not looking, but open to the right opportunity
In post, performing well and rarely on job boards. This is where much of the real quality lies.

The other 70% are the people who are doing well where they are. They're leading change programmes and growing fundraising income where they are, so they have no reason to browse vacancies. They'll only hear about Rowcroft if someone they can trust approaches them directly, explains the mission honestly and makes a considered case for why a role is worth a conversation.

That's the gap my approach is designed to fill, whenever it's useful to you. I keep an advertisement on CharityJob working, and then search the 70% directly, so the shortlist reflects the whole market rather than only the part of it that happens to be looking this month.

The search challenge

Two different searches, with one shared test: values and delivery

Both roles need someone who can challenge and lead with respect, in a cause-led, close-knit organisation. The transformation role needs people who can implement, not just advise, and the interim nature of the post asks candidates to weigh up a time-limited commitment. The fundraising role needs a manager who combines donor warmth with team leadership and numbers. In practice I'd look across these pools.

Transformation Lead

  • Transformation and process improvement leads from the NHS, health and social care — understand care environments and clinical teams; test commercial rigour and appetite for challenge
  • Operational excellence and change leaders from commercial sectors — strong on process re-engineering, automation and business cases; test their sensitivity to a patient-centred, values-led culture
  • Interim and programme leaders from other charities and public services — used to short, visible assignments; test hands-on delivery through to benefits realisation

Philanthropy and Fundraising Manager

  • Senior fundraisers in hospices and healthcare charities — close to the cause and its supporters; test team leadership and appetite for a broader remit
  • Relationship fundraisers ready for their first or next management post — strong on stewardship and major or legacy relationships; test people leadership and forecasting
  • Fundraising managers from other South West charities — know the regional supporter landscape; test fit with a hospice culture and the Torquay base

Because both roles ask for confident challenge alongside collaborative leadership, I'd test values and style carefully, and I'd be honest with every candidate about the nature of each post. I'd also make sure candidates from a wide range of backgrounds hear about the roles, and that every conversation is open, fair and consistent.

How I'd approach this search

A transparent, five-stage process

You would see how the search is progressing at every stage, and you'd have a documented, defensible process to show your colleagues and trustees.

Foundation and briefing

A briefing that goes beyond the job description. For each role, I'd agree with you what success looks like at 6, 12 and 24 months (for the interim role, the outcomes and benefits expected within the assignment), the essential experience and where you're open to transferable backgrounds, and how the role works with your leadership team. I'd also agree with you the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate. I'd also agree with you the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate.

Talent mapping and outreach

A structured mapping of the four pools, concentrating on the 70% who aren't on job boards. Each person is approached directly with an honest account of Rowcroft, the role and, for the interim post, the nature of the assignment. Any advertisement continues alongside, and anyone who responds to it is assessed against the same framework.

Weekly transparency

I'd meet you weekly throughout, covering both searches together. You'd see the talent pool as it's built: who has been identified, who has been approached, who has shown interest, and who has been ruled out and on what basis. Nothing happens behind closed doors, and you can challenge or redirect the search at any point.

Candidate assessment

I interview every candidate I progress against the agreed competencies, testing, for the transformation role, process redesign, automation, business cases and benefits delivery, and for the fundraising role, income growth, stewardship, team leadership and forecasting, with alignment with your values through real examples. You receive a written report on each, covering strengths, risks and development areas. Where helpful, a short practical exercise, such as a fictional scenario built to test priorities and judgement, never unpaid consultancy.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of three to five candidates for each role who are appointable on both capability and fit. I then support interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management, and stay in touch with the successful candidate before they start.
