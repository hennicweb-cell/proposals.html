Understanding Bristol & Weston Hospitals Charity

Bringing moments of joy, comfort and hope to hospitals across the region

Thank you for letting me introduce myself. I fully respect that you have your own routes for this appointment, and I'm not asking you to change anything. I've put this together so you can see how I work, in case it's ever useful.

Bristol & Weston Hospitals Charity raises funds for all 13 hospitals within the Bristol NHS Foundation Trust, bringing moments of joy, comfort and hope to the millions of patients and loved ones who pass through their doors each year and to the 15,000 NHS staff who care for them. So many of those people want to say thank you, whether by taking on a challenge, fundraising in memory or spreading the word to their community group.

Your 2024–25 reporting shows what that goodwill can achieve: £2.4m raised through fundraising, with community activity already contributing meaningfully, from the Big Bristol Abseil raising £41k to community fundraising groups raising over £33k. This role is about building on that momentum as part of your 2025–30 strategy, which aims to grow and diversify income.

The vacancy

Community Partnerships and Events Fundraising Officer

  • Relationships with community groups, supporters and local partners who want to give something back to your hospitals

Why an advert alone has limits

Only around 30% of the market is looking at any one time

At any given moment, roughly 30% of potential candidates are actively searching: reading job boards, setting up alerts and applying. Your advertisement will reach them, and some will be excellent.

Visible on job boards. This is who an advert reaches.
70%Not looking, but open to the right opportunity
In post, performing well and rarely on job boards. This is where much of the real quality lies.

The other 70% are the people who are doing well where they are. They're running community partnerships, delivering events and looking after supporters well, so they have no reason to browse vacancies. They'll only hear about Bristol & Weston Hospitals Charity if someone they can trust approaches them directly, explains the mission honestly and makes a considered case for why a role is worth a conversation.

That's the gap my approach is designed to fill, whenever it's useful to you. I keep an advertisement on CharityJob working, and then search the 70% directly, so the shortlist reflects the whole market rather than only the part of it that happens to be looking this month.

The search challenge

Warm relationships and polished event delivery in one person

The brief asks for someone who can build trusted local relationships and look after supporters with real warmth, and who can also deliver events with discipline and turn community participation into repeatable income. People who are strong at one often need testing on the other, and a car-owning, community-facing post limits the pool to people who can work across Bristol and the surrounding area. In practice I'd look across four pools:

  • Community and events fundraisers in health and hospice charities — close to the NHS and to supporters' motivations; test income delivery and appetite for a larger, more strategic charity
  • Challenge-event and sporting-event fundraisers — strong on events like the Bristol Half Marathon and runner support; test community-group and stewardship depth
  • Supporter care and community officers from other charities — strong on relationships and retention; test event logistics and delivery on the day
  • Events and community engagement professionals from outside the sector — from venues, arts, sport and local government, bringing delivery polish and local networks; test their understanding of fundraising income and supporter journeys

Events work often involves flexibility around evenings and weekends, so I'd be open with candidates about that at the first conversation. I'd make sure candidates from a wide range of backgrounds hear about the role, and that every conversation is open, fair and consistent.

How I'd approach this search

A transparent, five-stage process

You would see how the search is progressing at every stage, and you'd have a documented, defensible process to show your colleagues and trustees.

Foundation and briefing

A briefing that goes beyond the job description. I'd agree with you what success looks like at 6, 12 and 24 months, the current community and events portfolio, the balance between partnerships, events and supporter care, the essential experience and where you're open to transferable backgrounds, and how the role works with the fundraising, marketing and hospital teams. I'd also agree with you the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate.

Talent mapping and outreach

A structured mapping of the four pools, concentrating on the 70% who aren't on job boards. Each person is approached directly with an honest account of Bristol & Weston Hospitals Charity and the role. Any advertisement continues alongside, and anyone who responds to it is assessed against the same framework.

Weekly transparency

I'd meet you weekly throughout. You'd see the talent pool as it's built: who has been identified, who has been approached, who has shown interest, and who has been ruled out and on what basis. Nothing happens behind closed doors, and you can challenge or redirect the search at any point.

Candidate assessment

I interview every candidate I progress against the agreed competencies, testing relationship-building, event delivery, supporter care, income awareness and alignment with your values through real examples. You receive a written report on each, covering strengths, risks and development areas. Where helpful, a short practical exercise, such as planning a community event from a fictional scenario, never unpaid consultancy.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of three to five candidates who are appointable on both capability and fit. I then support interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management, and stay in touch with the successful candidate before they start.
