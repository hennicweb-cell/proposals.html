Understanding Julia's House

Magical moments and memories for children and families

Thank you to Mike for taking my call, and to the HR team for reading this. I fully respect that you have just advertised the role, and I'm not asking you to change anything. If the advert brings you the people you need, that's excellent. If it doesn't bring the quality you're hoping for, I'd be glad to offer another route.

Julia's House provides specialist care to children with life-limiting and complex conditions across Dorset and Wiltshire, at home, in the community and at its two hospices in Devizes and Corfe Mullen. The Devizes hospice is the only children's hospice building in Wiltshire, supporting families from Salisbury to Swindon, and care is free to the families who use it. In recent years the charity has also been recognised for the way it looks after its own people.

What stands out to me is how much of that care depends on community support. This Community Fundraiser will be the face of Julia's House across Wiltshire, at fetes, shows, festivals and markets, so the person you appoint needs warmth, energy and the confidence to represent the charity in public, as much as fundraising experience.

Why an advert alone has limits

Only around 30% of the market is looking at any one time

At any given moment, roughly 30% of potential candidates are actively searching: reading job boards, setting up alerts and applying. Your advertisement will reach them, and some will be excellent.

30%Actively looking
Visible on job boards. This is who an advert reaches.
70%Not looking, but open to the right opportunity
In post, performing well and rarely on job boards. This is where much of the real quality lies.

The other 70% are the people who are doing well where they are. They're building community support and representing their charities well where they are, so they have no reason to browse vacancies. They'll only hear about Julia's House if someone they can trust approaches them directly, explains the mission honestly and makes a considered case for why a role is worth a conversation.

That's the gap my approach is designed to fill, whenever it's useful to you. I keep an advertisement on CharityJob working, and then search the 70% directly, so the shortlist reflects the whole market rather than only the part of it that happens to be looking this month.

The search challenge

Outgoing, organised and happy to be out in the community

The brief asks for someone who can walk into a busy fete and start a conversation, and who can also plan carefully, keep the CRM accurate and follow up every contact. The pattern of evenings, weekends and travel across a large county will suit some people and not others, and a good advert may attract plenty of applicants who haven't yet been tested on these points. In practice I'd look across four pools:

  • Community and events fundraisers in hospices and health charities across the South West — know the supporter journey; test appetite for a Wiltshire patch and evening and weekend work
  • Customer-facing and events professionals from retail, hospitality, tourism and festivals — natural with the public; test their connection to the cause and their fundraising discipline
  • Community engagement officers and volunteer coordinators — strong on relationships and volunteers; test event planning and income focus
  • Fundraisers ready for a more community-based role — from desk-based or regional teams; test confidence speaking to groups and representing the charity publicly

Because the work involves families in some of the hardest circumstances, I'd give particular attention to warmth, empathy and resilience. I'd make sure candidates from a wide range of backgrounds hear about the role, and that every conversation is open, fair and consistent.

How I'd approach this search

A transparent, five-stage process

You would see how the search is progressing at every stage, and you'd have a documented, defensible process to show your colleagues and trustees.

Foundation and briefing

A briefing that goes beyond the job description. I'd agree with you what success looks like at 6 and 12 months, the events calendar and the patch, the working pattern including evenings, weekends and any part-time options, the essential experience and where you're open to transferable backgrounds, and how the role works with the Wiltshire team and volunteers. I'd also agree with you the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate.

Talent mapping and outreach

A structured mapping of the four pools, concentrating on the 70% who aren't on job boards. Each person is approached directly with an honest account of Julia's House and the role. Any advertisement continues alongside, and anyone who responds to it is assessed against the same framework.

Weekly transparency

I'd meet you weekly throughout. You'd see the talent pool as it's built: who has been identified, who has been approached, who has shown interest, and who has been ruled out and on what basis. Nothing happens behind closed doors, and you can challenge or redirect the search at any point.

Candidate assessment

I interview every candidate I progress against the agreed competencies, testing community engagement, event planning and delivery, speaking to groups, relationship building, CRM discipline, fundraising compliance and alignment with your values through real examples. You receive a written report on each, covering strengths, risks and development areas. Where helpful, a short practical exercise, such as planning attendance at a fictional community event, never unpaid consultancy.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of three to five candidates who are appointable on both capability and fit. I then support interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management, and stay in touch with the successful candidate before they start.
