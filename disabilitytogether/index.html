Understanding Disability Together

A user-led charity with more than 35 years of making a difference

Thank you for taking the time to read this. We haven't been in touch before, so I wanted to introduce myself properly. I fully respect that the Board has its own process for this appointment, and I'm not asking you to change anything. This is a short sample proposal built around your Chief Executive vacancy, so you can see how I work, in case it's ever useful.

Founded as Living Options Devon in 1990, Disability Together has worked for more than 35 years alongside Disabled and d/Deaf people to challenge barriers, champion rights and create opportunities for people to live independent, fulfilling lives. Its new name and identity, introduced in 2025 in collaboration with Disabled people, staff, volunteers and partners, expressed that existing purpose more clearly. The charity is user-led, which sets it apart from a conventional service provider, and its work spans independent advocacy, information and advice, Deaf-led wellbeing support, accessible counselling, and disability and Deaf awareness training delivered with people with lived experience.

The charity is also in a period of change. A planned Chief Executive transition is underway, following almost five years of leadership that saw the move from Living Options Devon to Disability Together, and the announced merger with Disability Focus is intended to create a broader, more resilient support offer. The incoming leader will build on a strong foundation while helping to shape a new long-term strategy.

Why an advert alone has limits

Only around 30% of the market is looking at any one time

At any given moment, roughly 30% of potential candidates are actively searching: reading job boards, setting up alerts and applying. Your advertisement will reach them, and some will be excellent.

30%Actively looking
Visible on job boards. This is who an advert reaches.
70%Not looking, but open to the right opportunity
In post, performing well and rarely on job boards. This is where much of the real quality lies.

At Chief Executive level the effect is even stronger. The other 70% are leaders who are doing well where they are, running services, leading teams and working with commissioners, so they have no reason to browse vacancies. They'll only hear about Disability Together if someone they can trust approaches them directly, explains the opportunity honestly and makes a considered case for a conversation.

That's the gap my approach is designed to fill, whenever it might be useful to you. I keep an advertisement on CharityJob working, and then search the 70% directly, so the shortlist reflects the whole market rather than only the part of it that happens to be looking this month.

The search challenge

Inclusive leadership, financial judgement and commissioning confidence in one person

The brief asks for a leader who shares power with people with lived experience, understands the economics of contracted services, and can steer an organisation through change, in the context of a Chief Executive transition and an announced merger. Candidates are often strong on some of these and need testing on the others. In practice I'd look across five pools:

  • CEOs of smaller disability, advocacy and community charities — strong on values and breadth; test scale, commissioning and financial rigour
  • Deputies, COOs and Directors of Services ready for a first CEO role — strong on operations and quality; test strategic and Board-level leadership
  • Leaders from user-led organisations and independent living services — strong on lived-experience leadership; test financial and contract management
  • Senior managers from health, social care and advice organisations — strong on commissioning relationships; test the move to an independent, user-led charity
  • Leaders from adjacent sectors — the advert welcomes different professional backgrounds; test values and capabilities carefully

I wouldn't automatically require previous CEO experience or a disability-sector job title. Equally, I wouldn't assume lived experience alone substitutes for the leadership requirements, or that a candidate without disclosed lived experience lacks understanding. I'd assess inclusive leadership through evidence: how people with lived experience influenced decisions, priorities or resources, and what changed as a result.

How I'd approach this search

A transparent, five-stage process

You would see how the search is progressing at every stage, and you'd have a documented, defensible process to show your Board.

Foundation and briefing

A joint briefing with the Chair and relevant trustees that goes beyond the job description. Together we'd agree what success looks like at 6, 12 and 24 months, what continuity the Board wants to preserve and what it expects the new Chief Executive to change, the position of the merger and the integration still to come, the current financial position and forecasts, how existing applicants are handled, and how people with lived experience take part in selection. We'd also agree the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate.

Talent mapping and outreach

A structured mapping of the five pools across Devon, the South West and beyond, concentrating on the 70% who aren't on job boards. Each person is approached directly with an honest account of Disability Together, its user-led purpose and the opportunity. Any advertisement continues alongside, and anyone who responds to it is assessed against the same framework.

Weekly transparency

We'd meet weekly throughout. You'd see the talent pool as it's built: who has been identified, who has been approached, who has shown interest, and who has been ruled out and on what basis. Nothing happens behind closed doors, and you can challenge or redirect the search at any point.

Candidate assessment

I interview every candidate I progress against seven areas: inclusive leadership, strategic delivery, financial judgement, change leadership, external relationships, quality and governance, and people leadership. For each I'd distinguish the candidate's personal contribution from wider organisational achievements. You receive a written report on each. For finalists I'd suggest a fictional scenario, not a free strategy: a contract renewal, limited resources and differing service-user priorities, testing financial judgement, inclusion and decision-making together.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of three to five candidates who are appointable on both capability and fit, with salary expectations and notice periods, and a conversation about what overlap with the outgoing Chief Executive might be possible. I then support interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management, and stay in touch with the successful candidate before they start.

An accessible selection process

Accessible by design, and shaped with Disabled and d/Deaf people

For an organisation like Disability Together, how the selection works matters as much as who is selected. I'd agree an accessible process at the outset, so that no one is put off before they've had the chance to show what they can do.

  • Clear information about every selection stage, in the formats candidates prefer
  • A confidential route to request adjustments, including for d/Deaf candidates
  • Accessible venues and communication arrangements throughout
  • Consistent, job-related assessment criteria applied to every candidate
  • Meaningful involvement of Disabled and d/Deaf people in selection
  • Clear expectations for any presentation or exercise
