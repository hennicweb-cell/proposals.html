Understanding Empire Fighting Chance

Boxing and psychology, helping young people make positive changes

Thank you for letting me introduce myself. I fully respect that you have your own process for this appointment, and I'm not asking you to change anything. I've put this together so you can see how I work, and I'd also like to share how I could support you more widely in future. I've recently recruited the finance lead for both Carers Support BSG and Headway Cardiff, and I'm about to begin a campaign for Thrive in Somerset, so this is a part of the market I know well and am working in now.

Empire Fighting Chance is a Bristol-based sport for development charity. It uses psychologically informed, non-contact boxing programmes to reach around 10,000 vulnerable young people every year, in its gym, in schools, and through a growing network of partner organisations and affiliated amateur boxing clubs in England and Wales. Its model, a fusion of boxing and psychology delivered by coaches and therapists that young people trust, is distinctive.

What stands out to me is that the charity has grown quickly, with income rising from £1.4m in 2021 to around £2.7m in the year to October 2025. The Head of Finance role is therefore a builder's role as much as a custodian's: someone who can design and embed the finance processes, controls and insight that let a growing charity scale with confidence, while sitting on the Senior Leadership Team.

The vacancy

Head of Finance

A hands-on leadership role that owns the charity's financial operations, reporting and controls, and gives the CEO, Senior Leadership Team and Board the insight to decide with confidence.

Part-time, ideally three days a week Member of the Senior Leadership Team Works with the CEO, Treasurer and Board

What the role delivers

  • Month-end close and monthly management accounts, with analysis of income and expenditure, restricted grants, cashflow and restricted, unrestricted and designated funds
  • A rolling forecast, annual budgeting and multi-year financial planning, plus modelling for new projects and funding bids
  • Finance systems, data quality and controls that are fit for purpose and scalable, and ownership of the year-end audit and statutory compliance, including SORP
  • The financial side of grant agreements, a schedule of grants by tenure, funder reports and close work with the fundraising team on the pipeline
  • Oversight of the external bookkeeper and payroll bureau, treasury operations and value for money from suppliers
Essential Desirable

What the person specification asks for

  • Proven experience at Head of Finance level or similar, ideally in the charity sector, with strong working knowledge of SORP, fund accounting and statutory reporting
  • Experience of month-end close, management accounts, budgeting and forecasting
  • Experience of designing and implementing finance processes and controls from scratch in a growing organisation
  • Strong Excel and financial modelling, and the ability to turn financial data into clear insight for non-finance audiences
  • A qualified accountant (CIMA, ACCA, ACA or equivalent), or qualified by experience with strong relevant experience; Xero and an FP&A tool such as Spotlight Reporting or Fathom are desirable
Why an advert alone has limits

Only around 30% of the market is looking at any one time

At any given moment, roughly 30% of potential candidates are actively searching: reading job boards, setting up alerts and applying. Your advertisement will reach them, and some will be excellent.

30%Actively looking
Visible on job boards. This is who an advert reaches.
70%Not looking, but open to the right opportunity
In post, performing well and rarely on job boards. This is where much of the real quality lies.

The other 70% are the people who are doing well where they are. They're leading finance in charities and organisations where they are, so they have no reason to browse vacancies. They'll only hear about Empire Fighting Chance if someone they can trust approaches them directly, explains the mission honestly and makes a considered case for why a role is worth a conversation.

That's the gap my approach is designed to fill, whenever it's useful to you. I keep an advertisement on CharityJob working, and then search the 70% directly, so the shortlist reflects the whole market rather than only the part of it that happens to be looking this month.

The search challenge

A qualified finance leader who has built systems, in three days a week

The brief asks for a qualified or experienced finance leader who can sit on the SLT, has designed finance processes from scratch, and is comfortable being hands-on in a small team, all on a part-time basis. That narrows the pool to part-time finance leaders, portfolio professionals, returners, and people combining the role with other work, so I'd look across four pools:

  • Heads of Finance and finance managers in growing charities — in youth, sport, housing and arts organisations; test SORP depth and the from-scratch systems experience
  • Part-time and portfolio finance directors — used to senior, time-limited roles; test hands-on delivery alongside strategic input
  • Commercial FP&A and management accountants seeking purpose-led work — strong on modelling and forecasting; test fund accounting and charity governance
  • Experienced interim finance leaders and returners — bring breadth and flexibility; test the currency of their systems knowledge, including Xero and reporting tools

Because the role is part-time, I'd be open with candidates about the working pattern and pro-rata salary from the first conversation. I'd make sure candidates from a wide range of backgrounds hear about the role, and that every conversation is open, fair and consistent.

How I'd approach this search

A transparent, five-stage process

You would see how the search is progressing at every stage, and you'd have a documented, defensible process to show your colleagues and trustees.

Foundation and briefing

A briefing that goes beyond the job description. I'd agree with you what success looks like at 6, 12 and 24 months, the balance between building new systems and hands-on delivery, the essential experience and where you're open to qualified-by-experience candidates, and how the role works with the CEO, Treasurer and Senior Leadership Team. I'd also agree with you the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate.

Talent mapping and outreach

A structured mapping of the four pools, concentrating on the 70% who aren't on job boards. Each person is approached directly with an honest account of Empire Fighting Chance and the role, including the part-time working pattern. Any advertisement continues alongside, and anyone who responds to it is assessed against the same framework.

Weekly transparency

I'd meet you weekly throughout. You'd see the talent pool as it's built: who has been identified, who has been approached, who has shown interest, and who has been ruled out and on what basis. Nothing happens behind closed doors, and you can challenge or redirect the search at any point.

Candidate assessment

I interview every candidate I progress against the agreed competencies, testing SORP and fund accounting, building systems and controls, forecasting and modelling, communicating financial insight to non-finance colleagues and alignment with the charity's values through real examples. You receive a written report on each, covering strengths, risks and development areas. Where helpful, a short practical exercise, such as reviewing a fictional restricted-fund position, never unpaid consultancy.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of three to five candidates who are appointable on both capability and fit. I then support interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management, and stay in touch with the successful candidate before they start.
