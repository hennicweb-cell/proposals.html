Why an advert alone has limits

Only around 30% of the market is looking at any one time

At any given moment, roughly 30% of potential candidates are actively searching: reading job boards, setting up alerts and applying. Your advertisement will reach them, and some will be excellent.

30%Actively looking
Visible on job boards. This is who an advert reaches.
70%Not looking, but open to the right opportunity
In post, performing well and rarely on job boards. This is where much of the real quality lies.

The other 70% are the people who are doing well where they are. They're running regular giving, lottery and legacy programmes where they are, so they have no reason to browse vacancies. They'll only hear about RLSS UK if someone they can trust approaches them directly, explains the mission honestly and makes a considered case for why a role is worth a conversation.

That's the gap my approach is designed to fill, whenever it's useful to you. I keep an advertisement on CharityJob working, and then search the 70% directly, so the shortlist reflects the whole market rather than only the part of it that happens to be looking this month.

The search challenge

A multi-product builder, who also manages suppliers and a team

The brief asks for someone who has worked across several individual giving products, can build supporter journeys and business cases from nothing, can procure and manage agencies and platforms, and can take on lottery compliance and a CRM like Donorfy. Candidates with all of that are rare, which is why Donorfy is advantageous rather than essential, and why I'd expect to meet some people who are stretching up into a first programme-building role. The hybrid pattern, with fortnightly travel to Worcester, suits candidates in the Midlands and the South West. In practice I'd look across four pools:

  • Individual giving managers and senior executives ready to step up — strong on regular giving and journeys; test breadth across lottery, legacy and in-memory, and appetite for building
  • Lottery and regular giving specialists from charities with established programmes — know the products and compliance; test comfort in a greenfield environment with no existing infrastructure
  • Digital, direct response and agency-side fundraisers moving in-house — strong on acquisition, testing and suppliers; test stewardship, legacy and supporter care
  • Fundraisers from water safety, sport, education, youth and community organisations — close to the mission and audiences; test multi-product experience and return-on-investment thinking

I'd make sure candidates from a wide range of backgrounds hear about the role, and that every conversation is open, fair and consistent.

How I'd approach this search

A transparent, five-stage process

You would see how the search is progressing at every stage, and you'd have a documented, defensible process to show your colleagues and trustees.

Foundation and briefing

A briefing that goes beyond the job description. I'd agree with you what success looks like at 6, 12 and 24 months, the investment budget for acquisition and testing, how firm the lottery plans are, the balance between strategy and hands-on delivery, the essential experience and where you're open to transferable backgrounds, and how the role works with you and the wider team. I'd also agree with you the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate.

Talent mapping and outreach

A structured mapping of the four pools, concentrating on the 70% who aren't on job boards. Each person is approached directly with an honest account of RLSS UK and the role. Any advertisement continues alongside, and anyone who responds to it is assessed against the same framework.

Weekly transparency

I'd meet you weekly throughout. You'd see the talent pool as it's built: who has been identified, who has been approached, who has shown interest, and who has been ruled out and on what basis. Nothing happens behind closed doors, and you can challenge or redirect the search at any point.

Candidate assessment

I interview every candidate I progress against the agreed competencies, testing multi-product individual giving, supporter journeys, return on investment and forecasting, supplier management, compliance and the mindset to build from the ground up, through real examples. You receive a written report on each, covering strengths, risks and development areas. Where helpful, a short practical exercise, such as outlining a first-year plan against a fictional budget, never unpaid consultancy.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of three to five candidates who are appointable on both capability and fit. I then support interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management, and stay in touch with the successful candidate before they start.
