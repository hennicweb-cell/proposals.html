Understanding The Wallich

Helping people out of homelessness and rough sleeping across Wales

Thank you for letting me introduce myself. I fully respect that you have your own process for this appointment, and I'm not asking you to change anything. I've put this together so you can see how I work, in case it's ever useful. I've recently recruited the finance lead for Carers Support BSG and Headway Cardiff, and I'm about to begin a campaign for Thrive in Somerset, so this is a part of the market I know well and am working in now.

The Wallich is Wales's largest homelessness and rough sleeping charity, supporting thousands of people each year through more than 100 services across the country, from rough sleeping and prevention to temporary accommodation and specialist support. With around 500 colleagues, income of about £20m, and a mix of commissioned services, grants and charitable income, the finance function is both large and complex.

What stands out to me is how much this role asks of one person: day-to-day leadership of a finance team of around ten, payroll for 500 or more employees, restricted and unrestricted fund accounting, treasury, and daily contact with the Chief Executive, the Trustees and the Executive team. It is also time-limited, as a 15-month fixed term or secondment, so the right candidate must be ready to make an impact quickly.

The vacancy

Financial Controller

Day-to-day financial leadership of The Wallich, so that all income and expenditure is managed systematically, prudently, legally and with probity.

15-month fixed term or secondment Reports to the Director of Business Innovation Accountable to the CEO

What the role delivers

  • Leadership of the finance function through the Head of Financial Accounts, Grants and Statutory Reporting, the Senior Business Partner and the Payroll Officer
  • Budgets, forecasts and monthly management accounts covering variances, cash flow and fund balances, with insight for the Executive team, Trustees and committees
  • Oversight of payroll for 500 or more employees, and accounting for restricted and unrestricted funds, grants and commissioned services
  • Statutory accounts, audit and compliance under the Charities SORP, plus financial controls, treasury and working capital, including Housing Benefit and aged debt
  • Business partnering with budget holders, full cost recovery analysis and financial appraisal of funding bids and contracts
Essential Desirable

What the person specification asks for

  • At minimum part-qualified under ACCA, ACA, CIMA or CIPFA, or a recognised international equivalent, and a degree or equivalent
  • Experience managing a finance function, or a significant part of one, in a large or complex organisation, with team leadership, budgeting, forecasting and financial modelling
  • Strong understanding of financial management, accounting practice and regulatory requirements, including charity law, GDPR and employment legislation
  • Experience of working with Boards or Trustees, and strong written and verbal communication
  • Desirable: fully qualified, charity or third-sector experience, Housing Benefit experience, project management, and experience of project assessment tools
Why an advert alone has limits

Only around 30% of the market is looking at any one time

At any given moment, roughly 30% of potential candidates are actively searching: reading job boards, setting up alerts and applying. Your advertisement will reach them, and some will be excellent.

30%Actively looking
Visible on job boards. This is who an advert reaches.
70%Not looking, but open to the right opportunity
In post, performing well and rarely on job boards. This is where much of the real quality lies.

The other 70% are the people who are doing well where they are. They're leading finance in complex organisations where they are, so they have no reason to browse vacancies. They'll only hear about The Wallich if someone they can trust approaches them directly, explains the mission honestly and makes a considered case for why a role is worth a conversation.

That's the gap my approach is designed to fill, whenever it's useful to you. I keep an advertisement on CharityJob working, and then search the 70% directly, so the shortlist reflects the whole market rather than only the part of it that happens to be looking this month.

The search challenge

A senior finance leader, ready to step in for a fixed period

The brief asks for someone who can lead a team, run a complex charity finance function with payroll, restricted funds and commissioned services, and engage confidently with the Executive and Trustees, all on a 15-month fixed term or secondment. People with that experience are usually in permanent roles, so the time-limited nature will suit some and deter others, which is why the secondment option matters. In practice I'd look across four pools:

  • Heads of Finance and finance managers in large charities and housing or social care providers — used to commissioned income and restricted funds; test the step up to a senior leadership remit
  • Senior finance leaders in housing associations, local authorities and health bodies in Wales and the South West — used to Housing Benefit, regulation and boards; test SORP and charity fund accounting
  • Experienced interim and contract finance leaders — comfortable with a fixed term and fast starts; test depth in a values-led culture
  • Finance leaders who could be seconded from larger organisations or partners — bring stretch and perspective; test their appetite for a hands-on controller role

Because the role works with people who have experienced homelessness and promotes your values and the Welsh language in its work, I'd give attention to alignment with The Wallich's values alongside technical strength. I'd make sure candidates from a wide range of backgrounds hear about the role, and that every conversation is open, fair and consistent.

How I'd approach this search

A transparent, five-stage process

You would see how the search is progressing at every stage, and you'd have a documented, defensible process to show your colleagues and trustees.

Foundation and briefing

A briefing that goes beyond the job description. I'd agree with you what success looks like at 6 and 12 months within the fixed term, the priorities for the finance team, what you'd want from the role in your own work, the essential experience and where you're open to part-qualified or transferable backgrounds, the secondment option, and how the role works with you, the Chief Executive and the Trustees. I'd also agree with you the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate.

Talent mapping and outreach

A structured mapping of the four pools, concentrating on the 70% who aren't on job boards. Each person is approached directly with an honest account of The Wallich and the role, including the fixed term and the secondment option. Any advertisement continues alongside, and anyone who responds to it is assessed against the same framework.

Weekly transparency

I'd meet you weekly throughout. You'd see the talent pool as it's built: who has been identified, who has been approached, who has shown interest, and who has been ruled out and on what basis. Nothing happens behind closed doors, and you can challenge or redirect the search at any point.

Candidate assessment

I interview every candidate I progress against the agreed competencies, testing finance leadership, SORP and restricted fund accounting, payroll and controls, treasury, business partnering with Trustees and alignment with your values through real examples. You receive a written report on each, covering strengths, risks and development areas. Where helpful, a short practical exercise, such as reviewing a fictional monthly management pack, never unpaid consultancy.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of three to five candidates who are appointable on both capability and fit. I then support interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management, and stay in touch with the successful candidate before they start.
