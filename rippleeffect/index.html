West Country roots, a global mission, and legacy support that is already entrusted to you

Ripple Effect, formerly Send a Cow, was founded in 1988 by West Country dairy farmers and now works alongside rural families in Burundi, Ethiopia, Kenya, Rwanda, Uganda and Zambia to improve food production, earn livelihoods and break the cycle of poverty. Its approach combines sustainable agriculture, enterprise, gender equality and community development, and its UK team is based in Bath.

Gifts in Wills are already a meaningful part of the picture. Legacy income was approximately £712,000 in the year to June 2025, up from approximately £599,000 the year before, within a charity with total income of £12.6m. That is an established programme, not a start-up one, so the appointment is about growing future support while protecting the relationships and estate income already entrusted to the charity.

The live vacancy

Legacy Officer

A role that sits between marketing, stewardship and administration, helping to grow future legacy support while caring properly for the gifts already pledged.

Permanent Part-time Bath-based team

What the role involves

  • Legacy campaigns and supporter journeys that inspire people to create lasting change through a gift in their Will
  • Stewardship of people who have pledged, or who have enquired about leaving, a gift
  • Handling legacy enquiries sensitively and promptly
  • Supporting estate administration, working with Finance on notifications and income expectations
  • Using CRM information to improve journeys and maintain accurate, permission-based records
My reading of the brief

What a strong candidate would bring

  • Hands-on experience of legacy marketing and supporter journeys, with an eye for what works
  • Empathy, discretion and sound ethical judgement when speaking to supporters and executors
  • Relevant casework experience, with good judgement on when specialist advice is needed
  • CRM discipline and clear reporting, with an understanding of intentions, notifications and income received
  • A collaborative approach across individual giving, communications, supporter engagement and Finance
Why an advert alone has limits

Only around 30% of the market is looking at any one time

At any given moment, roughly 30% of potential candidates are actively searching: reading job boards, setting up alerts and applying. Your advertisement will reach them, and some will be excellent.

In post, performing well and rarely on job boards. This is where much of the real quality lies.

The other 70% are the people who are doing well where they are. They're running legacy programmes, building pledger relationships and handling estates with care, so they have no reason to browse vacancies. Legacy specialists are a small, well-networked community, and they'll only hear about Ripple Effect if someone they can trust approaches them directly, explains the opportunity honestly and makes a considered case for a conversation.

That's the gap my approach is designed to fill, whenever it might be useful to you. I keep an advertisement on CharityJob working, and then search the 70% directly, so the shortlist reflects the whole market rather than only the part of it that happens to be looking this month.

The search challenge

Growing the future pipeline while protecting what is already entrusted

Legacy work needs both marketing flair and careful, compassionate administration, and few people are strong at both. It also needs a particular kind of care, because the relationships involve people at sensitive moments in their lives, and the programme has history and trust built under another name. As a part-time role, it's also an opportunity to attract experienced specialists who want flexibility. In practice I'd look across five pools:

  • Legacy Officers and Executives ready to take ownership — strong on supporter journeys; test appetite for shaping a programme
  • Legacy and in-memory fundraisers — strong on marketing and stewardship; test casework and estate administration
  • Individual Giving specialists with legacy experience — strong on data and campaigns; test sensitivity and one-to-one conversations
  • Legacy administrators and estates specialists — strong on casework and controls; test marketing and relationship skills
  • Fundraisers from international development and other relationship-led causes — strong on mission alignment; test legacy depth

I wouldn't make international-development experience an automatic requirement. Relevant legacy expertise, an understanding of the mission and the ability to work with Ripple Effect's supporters matter more. I'd also look at what candidates have personally influenced, rather than judging them only on cash received during their tenure, as legacy income can vary considerably from year to year.

How I'd approach this search

A transparent, five-stage process

You would see how the search is progressing at every stage, and you'd have a documented, defensible process to show your colleagues and trustees.

Foundation and briefing

A joint briefing that goes beyond the job description. Together we'd agree what success looks like at 6 and 12 months, the balance between marketing, stewardship and administration, how estate administration is handled, the marketing budget and the size of the existing pledger and enquirer pool, how the role works with Individual Giving, Supporter Engagement and Finance, and how existing applicants are handled. We'd also agree the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate.

Talent mapping and outreach

A structured mapping of the five pools across the South West and beyond, concentrating on the 70% who aren't on job boards. Each person is approached directly with an honest account of Ripple Effect, its heritage and the role. Any advertisement continues alongside, and anyone who responds to it is assessed against the same framework.

Weekly transparency

We'd meet weekly throughout. You'd see the talent pool as it's built: who has been identified, who has been approached, who has shown interest, and who has been ruled out and on what basis. Nothing happens behind closed doors, and you can challenge or redirect the search at any point.

Candidate assessment

I interview every candidate I progress against seven areas: programme development, legacy marketing, stewardship, administration, data and insight, financial understanding, and collaboration. You receive a written report on each. For finalists I'd suggest a short fictional scenario, not a free strategy: a limited budget, a small pool of existing pledgers, new enquiries, an estate-administration issue, and a supporter whose Will uses the former name, asking for priorities, a supporter journey and how they'd communicate sensitively.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A shortlist of three to five candidates who are appointable on both capability and fit, with salary expectations and notice periods. I then support interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management, and stay in touch with the successful candidate before they start.
