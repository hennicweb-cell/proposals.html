Understanding BOWRA Foundation

Helping people rebuild their lives after stroke and brain injury

Thank you for letting me introduce myself. I fully respect that you have your own process for this appointment, and I'm not asking you to change anything. I've put this together so you can see how I work, in case it's ever useful.

BOWRA Foundation was founded in 2019 by former Royal Marine Mark Bowra MBE. It supports people rebuilding their lives after stroke and acquired brain injury through practical support, purposeful activity and community, including adaptive sports and rehabilitation activities such as golf, water sports and surfing. Based in Poole, it is expanding its volunteer programme and its rehabilitation support across the region.

What stands out to me is the breadth of this role. The brief asks for a Chief Executive who can generate income, strengthen governance and safeguarding, and manage day-to-day delivery, as well as being the public face of the charity alongside you. It is a role for a leader who is as comfortable with the detail as with the ambition, in what the profile describes as a small, dynamic charity.

What the role delivers

Why an advert alone has limits

Only around 30% of the market is looking at any one time

At any given moment, roughly 30% of potential candidates are actively searching: reading job boards, setting up alerts and applying. Your advertisement will reach them, and some will be excellent.

30%Actively looking
Visible on job boards. This is who an advert reaches.
70%Not looking, but open to the right opportunity
In post, performing well and rarely on job boards. This is where much of the real quality lies.

The other 70% are the people who are doing well where they are. They're leading charities and growing income where they are, so they have no reason to browse vacancies. They'll only hear about BOWRA Foundation if someone they can trust approaches them directly, explains the mission honestly and makes a considered case for why a role is worth a conversation.

That's the gap my approach is designed to fill, whenever it's useful to you. I keep an advertisement on CharityJob working, and then search the 70% directly, so the shortlist reflects the whole market rather than only the part of it that happens to be looking this month.

The search challenge

An unusually broad chief executive, in a small charity

The brief asks for someone who can raise income personally, run the finances and operations day to day, strengthen governance and safeguarding, and build trust with people affected by stroke and brain injury. People who are strong at one or two of these often need testing on the others. The profile makes military knowledge and a personal connection to brain injury desirable, not essential, so I wouldn't limit the search to former military leaders or existing neurological-charity chief executives. A Poole base means the realistic pool is within reach of Dorset, alongside candidates with a credible commitment to relocate. In practice I'd look across four pools:

  • Chief executives of smaller disability, rehabilitation, community health and carers' charities — used to the breadth of a small charity; test income generation and appetite for a founder-led culture
  • Directors and deputies in larger charities who keep hands-on fundraising and operational responsibility — ready for a first chief executive role; test board experience and financial stewardship
  • Leaders from veterans' wellbeing, adaptive sport and community rehabilitation organisations — close to the community and the activities; test governance and income generation
  • Experienced small-charity chief executives within travelling distance of Poole — know the region; test fit with the charity's stage of growth

I'd treat these as the pools I'd search, not as a promise that suitable candidates are already waiting. I'd make sure candidates from a wide range of backgrounds hear about the role, and that every conversation is open, fair and consistent.

How I'd approach this search

A transparent, five-stage process

You would see how the search is progressing at every stage, and you'd have a documented, defensible process to show your colleagues and trustees.

Foundation and briefing

A briefing that goes beyond the job description. I'd agree with you what success looks like at 6, 12 and 24 months, the balance between fundraising and operational management, the support the chief executive has for finance, safeguarding and administration, the timetable, the selection panel and the essential experience, and how the role works with you and the Trustees. I'd also agree with you the competencies I'll use to assess every candidate.

Talent mapping and outreach

A structured mapping of the four pools, concentrating on the 70% who aren't on job boards. Each person is approached directly with an honest account of BOWRA Foundation and the role. Any advertisement continues alongside, and anyone who responds to it is assessed against the same framework.

Weekly transparency

I'd meet you weekly throughout. You'd see the talent pool as it's built: who has been identified, who has been approached, who has shown interest, and who has been ruled out and on what basis. Nothing happens behind closed doors, and you can challenge or redirect the search at any point.

Candidate assessment

I interview every candidate I progress against the agreed competencies, testing evidence of income generation, governance and safeguarding, hands-on financial and operational delivery and empathetic leadership, not job titles alone, through real examples. You receive a written report on each, covering strengths, risks and development areas. Where helpful, a short practical exercise, such as a fictional board report scenario, never unpaid consultancy.

Shortlist, selection & offer

A small, well-evidenced shortlist of three to five candidates who are appointable on both capability and fit, and who understand both BOWRA's purpose and the realities of the role. I then support interview design, candidate preparation, references and offer management, and stay in touch with the successful candidate before they start.
